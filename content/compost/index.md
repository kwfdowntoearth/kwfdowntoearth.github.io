# what is compost 
"mixture of ingredients used as plant fertilizer and to improve soil's physical, chemical, and biological properties" - wikipedia

# why we compost
- improve soil health and fertility (increases nutrient content and biodiversity of microbes)
- reduce dependence on chemical fertilisers 
- reduce waste in landfills (plant waste, food waste)


# ingredients (have been told it's like baking a cake!)
basically anything organic (which basically means derived from living things!) could technically go in!

## - carbon 
needed for energy; the microbial oxidation of carbon produces the heat required for other parts of the composting process. high carbon materials tend to be **brown** and **dry**. 

(nearly all dead plant and animal materials have both carbon and nitrogen in different amounts)

examples of high carbon things 
- fallen leaves
- wood chips 
- paper and cardboard

## - nitrogen 
needed to grow and reproduce more organisms to oxidize the carbon. high nitrogen materials tend to be **green and wet** or **colourful**! 

example of high nitrogen things
- fruit and veggie peels and scraps
- fresh leaves
- garden cuttings 
- (technically many things from animal carcasses to pre and post-consumer food waste can be used but we avoid these to avoid pests)

composting is most efficient with a carbon-to-nitrogen ratio of above 25:1. rapid composting is favoured by having a carbon-to-nitrogen ratio of about 30 carbon units or less. above 30, the substrate is nitrogen-starved. below 15, it is likely to outpas a portion of nitrogen as ammonia. 

## - oxygen
required for oxidising carbon, the decomposition process. aerobic bacteria need oxygen levels above 5% to perform the processes needed for composting. 

## - water 
water is necessary in the right amounts to maintain activity without causing locally anaerobic conditions. 

(air/water balance is critical to maintaining high temperatures)

# compost friends! 
fall into two broad categories: 
1. **chemical decomposers**: perform chemical processes on the organic waste 
2. **physical decomposers**: process waste into small pieces eg grinding, tearing, chewing and digesting. 

## chemical decomposers 
- **bacteria**: process carbon and nitrogen and excrete plant-available nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus and magnesium. depending on the phase of composting, mesophilic or thermophilic bacteria may be the most important. 
    - **mesophilic bacteria** get compost to the themophilic stage through oxidation of organic material. makes fresh compost more bioavailable for plants. 
    - **thermophilic bacteria** activate once the mesophilic bacteria have begun to break down organic matter and increase temperature to their optimal range. thrive at higher temperatures, reaching 40 to 60C in typical mixes. 
    - **actinomycetota** are needed to break down paper products such as newspaper, bark etc and other large molecules such as lignin and cellulose that are more difficult to compose. the reason for the "pleasant, earthy smell of compost" :) they make carbon, ammonia and nitrogen nutrients available to plants. 
    - **fungi** such as 

- earthworms 
- black soldier flies 
- flies 
- ants 
- snails 
- slugs

## how we compost at KWF  
1. trench composting 
- just an accelerated version of what soil already does to fallen organic matter.
- soil column’s natural drainage and earthworm activity instead
- less visible and less labour that hot composting

2. hot compost 
- higher yield 
- more efficient and faster 
- recommended one cubic metre of material from the outset; larger piles reach higher temperatures and remain in a thermophilic stage for days or weeks
